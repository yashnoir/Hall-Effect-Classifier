How it works: Hall Effect theory & formulas

The Hall Effect

When a current I flows through a conductor or semiconductor placed in a perpendicular magnetic field B, the Lorentz force deflects the charge carriers toward one side of the sample. This charge separation builds up a transverse electric field, which produces a measurable voltage VH across the sample, known as the Hall voltage.

Step 1: Hall Coefficient

RH = (VH · w) / (I · B)

The Hall coefficient RH characterizes the sign and density of the charge carriers. Here w is the sample thickness in the direction of the magnetic field, I is the applied current, and B is the magnetic flux density.

Step 2: Carrier Concentration

n = 1 / (|RH| · e)

For a single dominant carrier type, the carrier concentration n is inversely proportional to |RH|. The elementary charge used is e = 1.602 × 10−19 C.

Step 3: Classification (Sign of RH)

  • RH < 0: N-type semiconductor. Majority carriers are electrons.
  • RH > 0: P-type semiconductor. Majority carriers are holes.
  • RH = 0: Intrinsic or undetermined. No net Hall response was measured.
